    Preparing Images Before the Analysis¶

    Time series algorithms assume that a pixel means the same thing on every date. These functions take out what does not: images that sit a pixel or two off, view and sun geometry, bands that differ between sensors, and resolution. Most come from FORCE (David Frantz, GPL-3.0-or-later); each one says where it is from, when to use it and how to call it.

    Problem Function
    The dates do not line up with a reference image coregister, coregister_local
    The view angle changes the reflectance from date to date nbar (with sentinel2_angles)
    Landsat and MODIS next to Sentinel-2 spectral_adjust
    Brightness, greenness and wetness instead of bands tasseled_cap
    The 20 m bands of Sentinel-2 at 10 m sentinel2_resmerge
    A coarse product, or Landsat, at the pixels of Sentinel-2 improphe
    Multispectral bands at the pixels of a finer panchromatic band pansharpen

    For the atmospheric correction itself, from a Level 1C scene, see Sentinel-2 Level 1C to Surface Reflectance. For Landsat and Sentinel-2 in one series where HLS covers the dates, see Pre-processing.

    Coregistration¶

    When. Images of the same place that are a fraction of a pixel to a few pixels apart make a stable surface look like change. coregister finds each date's shift to a reference image by phase correlation and moves the date; coregister_local lets the shift vary across the scene, for a misregistration that grows from one side to the other. Both port the co-registration of AROSICS (Scheffler et al. 2017, Remote Sensing 9(7):676; AROSICS 1.14.0, Apache-2.0).

    The cube and the reference must be on the same pixel grid (the same projected CRS and cell size, the cells a whole number apart): zeit.load_raster(..., like=cube) puts the reference there.

    reference = zeit.load_raster("reference.tif", like=cube)
aligned, shifts = zeit.coregister(cube, reference, band="nir", ref_band="nir")
shifts[["x_shift_px", "y_shift_px", "reliability", "ssim_before", "ssim_after", "success"]]

aligned, points = zeit.coregister_local(cube, reference, grid_res=100, window_size=128,
                                        band="nir", ref_band="nir")

    A date that fails (no overlap of valid data, no clear match, a shift above max_shift, 5 pixels by default) stays as it was, with success=False and the reason in the table. Check ssim_before and ssim_after to see that the date agrees better afterwards, and min_reliability refuses the weak shifts.

    For a single Level 1C scene, level2 takes reference= and coregisters the TOA reflectance with the global coregister before the atmospheric correction, as FORCE does with its own method.

    NBAR¶

    When. The reflectance of a pixel depends on the angles of the sun and the sensor. nbar normalises it to a nadir view and a chosen sun zenith with the c-factor method of Roy et al. (2016, 2017), the view-angle step of HLS. It needs the per-pixel SZA, SAA, VZA and VAA; for Sentinel-2, sentinel2_angles makes them from each tile's MTD_TL.xml. Written from the papers and the HLS User Guide, not from FORCE's code.

    angles = zeit.sentinel2_angles(["T1/MTD_TL.xml", "T2/MTD_TL.xml"], cube)   # one XML per date of cube.time
cube_nbar = zeit.nbar(cube, angles)
cube_nbar = zeit.bandpass_adjust(cube_nbar)     # HLS's order: view angles first, then the bandpass

    The red edge parameters are an interpolation the paper found not particularly suited to that region, and the parameters are MODIS's snow-free ones: mask snow first and inspect return_cfactor=True where the geometry is extreme.

    Spectral adjustment¶

    When. To put Landsat 4 to 9 and MODIS (Terra, Aqua) in the bands of Sentinel-2A, including the red edge bands that those sensors do not measure. spectral_adjust is FORCE's spectral adjustment (Scheffler, Frantz and Segl 2020, Remote Sensing of Environment 241:111723). It is not bandpass_adjust, which takes Sentinel-2 towards Landsat 8's OLI.

    s2like = zeit.spectral_adjust(landsat, sensor="landsat-8")      # blue ... swir22, plus rededge1-3 and nir08
ndvi = zeit.compute_indices(s2like, ["NDVI"])                   # nir is B08, as on Sentinel-2

    With force_search=True the arithmetic is FORCE's to the letter; by default one search is done as in the paper, which changes 1.6-2.3 % of the observations of synthetic spectra beyond one unit of integer reflectance (see the benchmark).

    Tasseled Cap¶

    When. Brightness, greenness and wetness are three weighted sums of six bands, a compact input for change detection. tasseled_cap does by default what FORCE's TSA does, with Crist's (1985) matrix for every sensor.

    tc = zeit.tasseled_cap(landsat)                                       # as FORCE
tc = zeit.tasseled_cap(landsat, ["wetness"], coefficients="sensor")   # the matrix of each date's sensor
lt = zeit.landtrendr(tc.sel(band="wetness"))

    Only Crist's matrix was derived for surface reflectance; the others were derived for top-of-atmosphere reflectance, so applying them to surface reflectance is common practice but approximate. "disturbance" is FORCE's TC-DI (brightness - greenness - wetness), not the standardized disturbance index of Healey et al. (2005).

    The 20 m bands at 10 m¶

    When. The six 20 m bands of Sentinel-2 (red edge 1-3, B8A, SWIR1, SWIR2) on the 10 m grid, predicted from the 10 m green, red and broad NIR. sentinel2_resmerge is FORCE's resolution merge. The input is a cube on the 10 m grid where each 20 m pixel is four equal pixels. For a single Level 1C scene, level2 does it itself with merge=, on the TOA reflectance before the atmospheric correction as FORCE does; use sentinel2_resmerge for cubes that are already corrected or for time series.

    sharp = zeit.sentinel2_resmerge(s2, method="regression", clear=cloud_free)
fast = zeit.sentinel2_resmerge(s2, method="improphe", clear=cloud_free)    # faster

    clear is True where a pixel is not cloud or shadow; the others are not predicted. FORCE's third option, STARFM, is not included.

    ImproPhe¶

    When. To sharpen a coarse field (a 500 m phenology metric, say) or the scenes of a coarser sensor to the pixels of a finer one. improphe gives each pixel the weighted mean of the coarse values at the neighbours that look like it in the fine data. It is FORCE's CF-ImproPhe and L2-ImproPhe (Frantz et al. 2016, IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing 54(7):4153-4164).

    # a yearly 500 m field (year, y, x), with the Sentinel-2 cube of the same years
sharp = zeit.improphe(lsp_500m, sentinel2, by="year", kernel_size=500, texture_size=60)
# Landsat scenes (time, band, y, x) at 10 m from the same cube
l8_10m = zeit.improphe(landsat, sentinel2, by=None, kernel_size=300, texture_size=60)

    The default radii (kernel_size=2500, texture_size=330, in map units) suit 10 m pixels. Everything is in memory, with a circle of kernel_size around each pixel: for a large area, work a tile at a time with a margin of that size.

    Pansharpening¶

    When. To put multispectral bands on the grid of a finer panchromatic band, for any sensor, band count and resolution ratio above 1 (Landsat 8's band 8, Sentinel-2's 10 m with its 20 m or 60 m bands, the CBERS-4A WPM). pansharpen has four methods, "gsa" (the default), "brovey", "hpf" and "pca".

    fused = zeit.pansharpen(wpm_ms, wpm_pan, method="gsa")                       # CBERS-4A WPM, ratio 4
fused = zeit.pansharpen(l8_ms, l8_b8, "gsa", intensity_bands=["B3", "B4"])   # Landsat 8: the pan sees green and red

    The formulas were written from the literature without the papers at hand, and the defaults of "hpf" are Zeit's rather than the paper's; treat them as a starting point. Bands that the pan does not see (NIR, SWIR for a visible pan) get little detail from GSA, and intensity_bands chooses the bands that estimate the intensity. The registration of the pan to the bands is not checked: use coregister first.
